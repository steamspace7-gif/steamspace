Introduction & Safety

Students use a laser cutter to precisely cut their puzzle designs from approved materials. A laser cutter uses a focused beam of light (a laser) to vaporize, melt, or burn away material along the vector paths drawn in Inkscape. Because the beam is powerful enough to cut through wood, acrylic, and paper, everyone in the room must follow strict safety rules.

Laser Cutter Safety Rules

  • Safety glasses: Wear approved laser safety glasses whenever the machine is cutting, even if the door is closed. The laser can reflect off surfaces and damage eyes.
  • Ventilation: Keep the exhaust fan running and the room well ventilated. Cutting releases fumes and small particles.
  • Fire safety: A water bucket, fire blanket, or extinguisher rated for electrical fires must be nearby. Never leave the laser running unattended. Watch for sparks or flames.
  • Approved materials only: Cut only materials the instructor has approved. Common approved materials are plywood, MDF, cardboard, paper, and cast acrylic.
  • No PVC or vinyl: Never laser-cut PVC, vinyl, or any unknown plastic. These release toxic chlorine gas that can damage the machine and harm people.
  • Materials safety: Know what your material is made of. If you are unsure, ask the instructor. Oil-coated, painted, or chemically treated materials can release dangerous fumes.
  • Emergency stop: Know where the laser’s stop button is and how to use it.

When everyone follows the rules, laser cutting is a safe and powerful way to turn a digital drawing into a real object.

Arizona 6th Grade Educational Standards

This field trip aligns with Arizona Science and Mathematics Standards for 6th grade.

Physical Sciences

6.P1U1.3: Develop and use models to further understanding that matter is made of small particles called atoms, and that pure substances are made of only one type of atom or molecule. Students can connect the laser beam to concentrated energy: the laser transfers energy to the material, causing atoms and molecules at the cut line to vibrate rapidly, heat up, and separate — changing phase from solid to gas or liquid.

Energy and Technology

6.P4U2.5: Investigate how technological devices use energy. Students investigate how a laser cutter converts electrical energy into highly concentrated light energy, then into thermal energy that removes material. Discuss efficiency, power settings, and why some materials cut more easily than others.

Engineering Design (Middle School)

MS-ETS1-1: Define the criteria and constraints of a design problem with sufficient precision to ensure a successful solution. Students define their puzzle’s size (in millimeters), number of pieces, shape of pieces, artwork theme, and material. Constraints include laser-bed size, material thickness, and safety rules.

MS-ETS1-2: Evaluate competing design solutions using a systematic process to determine how well they meet the criteria and constraints of the problem. Students compare different puzzle layouts in Inkscape, check that pieces fit, and select the design that best meets the criteria before cutting.

Mathematics

6.G.A.1: Find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals, and polygons by composing into rectangles or decomposing into triangles and other shapes. Students draw polygonal puzzle pieces and artwork in Inkscape, then can decompose complex shapes to estimate or verify their areas.

6.G.A.3: Draw polygons in the coordinate plane given coordinates for the vertices; use coordinates to find the length of a side joining points with the same first coordinate or the same second coordinate. Students position shapes on the Inkscape coordinate plane and use x- and y-coordinates to align, size, and space puzzle pieces.

Sources: Arizona Science Standards (ADE), NGSS Middle School Engineering Design, and Arizona Mathematics Standards

Inkscape Introduction

Inkscape is a free vector drawing program. Vector drawings stay sharp when scaled and are perfect for laser engraving and cutting. Students draw shapes, add text, color, and patterns to create logos, posters, stickers, and puzzle art.

Things to Know Before You Get Started

What is Inkscape? What size is your puzzle in millimeters? How will the laser show your picture? The laser uses an SVG (Scalable Vector Graphic) file. Overview video: SVG & laser basics.

Drawing Circles, Squares, Rectangles, Triangles, and Other Shapes

Practice drawing shapes with Inkscape’s shape tools: Inkscape shapes video.

Adding Text to Your Art

Using the Laser Cut Jigsaw Extension

In Inkscape: Extensions → Render → Lasercut Jigsaw. Set width and height in millimeters, corner radius, and pieces across/down, then apply. Walkthrough: Lasercut Jigsaw video.

Saving Your SVG and Uploading to the Laser Machine

Save as Plain SVG (*.svg) to your Downloads folder with your name as the filename. Then upload the artwork to the laser machine for cutting.

Using Acrylic Pens to Color Your Puzzle

Acrylic paint pens offer pen precision with a paint finish. They're beginner-friendly, work on many surfaces, and dry quickly for easy layering. Use them like a paintbrush rather than a marker.

Reflect & Share

You designed, cut, and colored a puzzle today. Now take time to reflect on the engineering and artistic choices you made, and learn from the puzzles your classmates created.

Discussion Questions

  • What shapes, patterns, or cultural artwork inspired your puzzle design?
  • How did you decide on the size and number of puzzle pieces?
  • What was one challenge you faced in Inkscape? How did you solve it?
  • How does a laser cutter turn a digital SVG file into a real object?
  • What happened when the laser beam hit the material? What kind of energy changed?
  • Why is it important to know what material you are cutting before you start the laser?
  • If you redesigned your puzzle, what would you change?

Gallery Walk

Place your finished puzzle on your desk. Walk around the room and look at each classmate's puzzle. Use sticky notes or a comment sheet to leave one "glow" (something you like) and one "grow" (a thoughtful suggestion) for at least two puzzles.

Trade-and-Solve

Trade puzzles with a partner. Try to assemble each other's puzzle without seeing the original design. Notice how the piece shapes, color, and image edges help you figure out where each piece belongs. After solving, give feedback: Which pieces were easiest to place? Which were hardest?

Exit Ticket

On a scrap piece of paper or index card, answer these two questions before leaving:

  1. Name one safety rule for laser cutting and explain why it matters.
  2. Name one 6th-grade standard this project connects to and give a specific example from your puzzle.
