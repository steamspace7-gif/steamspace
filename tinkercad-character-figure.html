6th Grade

Tinkercad Character Figure

Design an original 3D character in Tinkercad, master volume math, and export a print-ready STL for our fleet of Bambu Lab 3D printers — a 3-hour hands-on field trip at Building 106.

Students use Tinkercad — a free, browser-based 3D design tool from Autodesk — to build a small original character from geometric primitives. They learn to place, move, size, and group shapes, then check their model against a real-world constraint: the bounding-box volume must be ≤ 20 cm³. Finally, they export an STL file that staff queue on our six Bambu Lab P1S 3D printers. Success today is a completed design and a valid STL — prints may finish after the block.

Introduction & Safety

Students design original characters in Tinkercad using 3D shapes (boxes, cylinders, spheres). The class theme is chosen by the teacher — past themes include animals, heroes, and cultural figures. Students build from primitives, group their parts into one solid model, and export an STL file for 3D printing. Our workshop has six Bambu Lab P1S FDM printers that staff operate; students focus on design, not printer operation.

Safety Rules

  • Adults only operate the 3D printers — no exceptions. Printers have hot nozzles (up to 300 °C) and heated beds.
  • Stay clear of printer moving parts during operation. Watch from behind the safety line.
  • Burn hazard: never touch a printer nozzle or heated bed — they stay hot long after a print finishes.
  • Approved filament only: staff loads PLA or PETG. Students do not handle filament spools.
  • Original characters only: no franchise or copyrighted mascots. Design your own!
  • Save your work: export your STL before leaving. Staff will queue your print after the session.

What Is 3D Design? (Elementary)

From Shapes to Characters

3D design means building objects in three dimensions — length, width, and height — on a computer. Tinkercad lets you drag shapes onto a workplane and combine them. A box becomes a body. A cylinder becomes a leg. A sphere becomes a head. When you group shapes together, they become one solid model that the 3D printer can print.

What Is 3D Printing?

A 3D printer builds objects layer by layer from the bottom up. It melts plastic filament and squeezes it through a hot nozzle, tracing each layer like drawing with a hot glue gun. Our Bambu Lab P1S printers use FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) — the same technology used in professional makerspaces and engineering labs. Students design; staff prints.

What Is an STL File?

STL (STereoLithography) is a file format that describes the surface of a 3D model as a mesh of triangles. When you export from Tinkercad, your grouped shapes become one STL file. The printer's slicing software reads the STL and figures out how to print it layer by layer. STL files don't carry color — the print is one filament color (staff can swap colors between prints).

Classroom Phrases

  • "Group makes it one" — grouped shapes export as a single solid part.
  • "Volume = L × W × H" — multiply the three edge lengths to check the size rule.
  • "Export STL for 3D print" — the file type that tells the printer what to build.
  • "Design is success" — a valid STL is a win, even if the print finishes later.

Sources: Autodesk Tinkercad Learn (official), Wikipedia STL/FFF, Bambu Lab P1S product page

The Size Rule — Volume ≤ 20 cm³

Every character must fit inside a bounding box whose volume is 20 cm³ or less. This keeps prints fast and gives every student a turn on the printer fleet.

How to Check

  1. Select your whole grouped character in Tinkercad.
  2. Read the length, width, and height from the dimensions inspector.
  3. Convert all three to centimeters (10 mm = 1 cm).
  4. Multiply: Volume = L × W × H. Compare to 20.
ExampleDimensionsVolumeResult
Small figure2.5 × 2.5 × 3.0 cm18.75 cm³✅ Pass
Cube3.0 × 3.0 × 3.0 cm27 cm³❌ Fail — shrink it
Tall thin figure2.0 × 2.0 × 5.0 cm20 cm³✅ Pass (right at limit)

Rough intuition: about a 2.7 cm cube (∛20 ≈ 2.7). Tall thin figures can still fail if the product of all three edges is too large. Record your numbers on the design checklist!

Source: STEAMSPACE site constraint (not an Autodesk rule)

MS-ETS1-2: Evaluate competing design solutions using a systematic process to determine how well they meet the criteria and constraints. Students compare design choices during mid-check — thicker vs thinner limbs, standing vs lying orientation — and revise to meet the size rule.

MS-ETS1-4: Develop a model to generate data for iterative testing and modification of a proposed object, tool, or process. The Tinkercad model itself is the design prototype; the volume check and print-readiness checklist are the iterative test cycle.

6.RP.A.3d: Use ratio reasoning to convert measurement units. Students convert Tinkercad's millimeter readings to centimeters for the volume calculation (10 mm = 1 cm).

Build Your Character

Step 1: Join & Warm Up

Join the Tinkercad Class with your teacher's class code or link. Try the official Learn the Moves lesson to practice placing, moving, rotating, and sizing shapes.

Step 2: Sketch Your Idea

On paper, sketch 2–3 quick thumbnails of your character. Pick your favorite. List the body parts you'll need: head, body, arms, legs, eyes, a prop? Keep it simple — you're building from blocks and cylinders, not sculpting.

Step 3: Build from Primitives

Start with a body box or cylinder. Add a head. Add limbs — keep them thick, not spindly, because the printer needs chunky parts to work well. Use the Group tool to combine shapes into one solid. Check your dimensions often!

Step 4: Check the Size Rule

Select your whole grouped figure. Read L, W, H from the dimensions box. Convert to cm (divide mm by 10). Multiply: L × W × H. Is it ≤ 20 cm³? If not, shrink some parts and check again.

Step 5: Print-Readiness Check

Look at your model from all sides (rotate the view). Are there floating parts with nothing underneath? Thin walls that might snap? An unstable base? Fix these before exporting. Complete the print-readiness checklist.

Step 6: Export STL

Press Export on the top toolbar. Under For 3D Print, choose STL. Save the file with a clear name like Firstname-Character-Theme.stl. Submit it to your teacher's queue — staff will print it on the Bambu Lab P1S!

Design Tips & Common Mistakes

Build Tips (Theme-Agnostic)

  • Start big, then shrink: build your character first, then scale down to meet the 20 cm³ rule.
  • Thicker is better: FDM printers need chunky parts. Avoid paper-thin walls and hairline limbs.
  • Keep it standing: a figure with a flat, stable base prints more easily than one that needs lots of support.
  • Group before export: ungrouped shapes export as separate pieces. Group everything into one solid!
  • No floating parts: unsupported islands in mid-air will fail or need supports. Connect everything.
  • Original characters only: no franchise mascots or copyrighted characters. Design your own!

What Students Learn

  • 3D Design: Build objects from geometric primitives in a browser-based CAD tool.
  • Volume & Measurement: Compute bounding-box volume (L × W × H) and convert between mm and cm — real math with a real constraint.
  • Engineering Design Cycle: Plan, build, test (size check), revise, and export — the same cycle professional engineers use.
  • Digital Fabrication: Understand how an STL file becomes a physical object through FDM 3D printing.
  • Constraints & Optimization: Design creatively within a measurable size limit — creativity meets engineering.
  • Originality & IP: Create original characters, not copies of franchise mascots — respect intellectual property.
