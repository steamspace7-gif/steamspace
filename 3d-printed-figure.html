6th Grade

3D Printed Figure

Design an original character in Tinkercad and take home a figure printed on our Bambu Lab 3D printers — a 3-hour hands-on field trip at Building 106.

Students design an original character from 3D shapes in Tinkercad — a free, browser-based design tool — then export their model for our fleet of Bambu Lab 3D printers. Every student takes home a printed figure!

Schedule This Field Trip

Introduction & Safety

Students build original characters from geometric shapes — boxes, cylinders, spheres — and group them into one solid model. The class theme is chosen by the teacher: past themes include animals, heroes, and cultural figures.

Safety Rules

  • Adults only operate the 3D printers — they have hot nozzles and heated beds.
  • Watch from behind the safety line — stay clear of moving parts during a print.
  • Never touch a printer nozzle or heated bed — they stay hot after a print finishes.
  • Original characters only — no franchise mascots. Design your own!
  • Export your STL before you leave — staff print it and deliver the figures to your school.

What We Use

  • Laptops or Chromebooks with Tinkercad — free in the browser, class code provided
  • Bambu Lab 3D printers — staff-operated, PLA filament
  • Paper sketch sheet to plan your character before you build

New to Tinkercad? Start with the official Learn center lessons.

What Is 3D Design?

3D design means building objects in three dimensions — length, width, and height — on a computer. In Tinkercad you drag shapes onto the workplane and combine them: a box becomes a body, a cylinder becomes a leg, a sphere becomes a head. When you group shapes, they become one solid model the printer can print.

What Is 3D Printing?

A 3D printer builds an object layer by layer from the bottom up — like drawing with a hot glue gun, one thin layer at a time. Your finished design becomes an STL file, the file type that tells the printer what to build.

Classroom Phrases

  • "Group makes it one" — grouped shapes print as a single solid part.
  • "Export STL for 3D print" — the file type that tells the printer what to build.
  • "Design is success" — a finished design is a win, even if the print finishes after you leave.

Build Your Figure

Step 1: Join & Warm Up

Join the Tinkercad class with your teacher's class code. Try placing, moving, rotating, and sizing shapes.

Step 2: Sketch Your Idea

On paper, sketch 2–3 quick thumbnails of your character. Pick your favorite and list the body parts you'll need: head, body, arms, legs, eyes, a prop? Keep it simple — you're building from blocks and cylinders, not sculpting.

Step 3: Build from Shapes

Start with a body box or cylinder. Add a head. Add limbs — keep them thick, not spindly, because the printer needs chunky parts. Use the Group tool to combine everything into one solid. Keep your character small — about the size of a 3 cm cube — so it prints quickly for everyone.

Step 4: Print-Readiness Check

Look at your model from all sides. Are there floating parts with nothing underneath? Thin walls that might snap? An unstable base? Fix these before exporting.

Step 5: Export STL

Press Export, choose STL, and save with a clear name like Firstname-Character.stl. Staff will print your figure on the Bambu Lab printers!

Design Tips & Common Mistakes

Build Tips

  • Thicker is better: printers need chunky parts. Avoid paper-thin walls and hairline limbs.
  • Keep it standing: a flat, stable base prints more easily.
  • Group before export: ungrouped shapes print as separate pieces. Group everything!
  • No floating parts: connect everything to something solid.

Common Mistakes

MistakeFix
Forgot to group — export makes separate piecesSelect all → Group before Export
Hairline limbs — the printer can't print thin featuresThicken limbs
Floating parts — unsupported islands failConnect parts to the body

AZ 6th Grade Standards

Students design an original 3D figure in Tinkercad, check it for print-readiness, and export an STL file for 3D printing — touching Arizona middle-school engineering design (MS-ETS1), 6th grade geometry and measurement, speaking/listening, and educational technology standards for design processes and digital artifact creation.

What Students Learn

  • 3D Design: Build objects from geometric shapes in a browser-based design tool.
  • Engineering Design Cycle: Plan, build, check, revise, and export.
  • Digital Fabrication: See how a design on screen becomes a real object, layer by layer.
  • Originality: Create original characters, not copies — creativity within the rules.
